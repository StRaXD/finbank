# PRD Quality Review — Finbank

Calibration: this is a lightweight, frontend-only PRD whose job is to turn 15 Figma frames into a working SPA on mock data. It sits at the top of a chain (UX, then architecture, then stories), so the bar is: are done-ness and the data contract clear enough to build and review against? Market research, multiple personas and user journeys are not expected and were not penalised.

## Overall verdict
The PRD does its job well. It has a clear two-part thesis ("pixel-faithful" plus "swap the data layer later"), a counter-metric that stops the first part from undermining the second, a single "Mock today" anchor for all dates, and unusually honest handling of design gaps through `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, an explicit §8.2 deferred list and per-FR "shown but inert" notes. The main risks are in the data contract and in testability. How Expense items relate to Transactions, how liability accounts add into Total Balance, whether mock edits persist, and how a logarithmic axis can show a "$0" tick are all left undefined. The fidelity bar that SM-1 and NFR-1 rest on has no tolerance, so "done" for the core metric comes down to reviewer opinion.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Decisions are written as decisions, not as "considerations." §8.1 records nine resolved calls, each with a direction: the logarithmic y axis, copy errors corrected in favour of internal consistency (FR-21 "The design's 'This Week / Last Week' is treated as a copy error"; FR-24 "'Mar 2022' is treated as a copy error"), and limits only with no over-limit indicator (FR-26). Trade-offs say what was given up. FR-26 drops the over-limit comparison "to stay faithful to Figma", and FR-33 accepts that "Mock values may differ from the Figma placeholders". §8.2 items are genuinely open and each has an owner ("owner: product/design").

The only weak spot is the bar-chart axis decision. It is stated firmly but cannot be built as written (see Done-ness).

### Findings
- **[low]** No `[NOTE FOR PM]` at the one real product tension (§2.1 vs FR-26) — The JTBD promises "track my progress against [spending targets]", but FR-26 deliberately does not compare the limit with actual spending. The PRD resolves this silently in favour of fidelity. *Fix:* Narrow the JTBD to "set spending targets" for v1, or add a NOTE FOR PM recording that this job is only half-served until §8.2 #6 lands.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is no persona, innovation or NFR theater. The single persona carries one concrete fact (the sample user "Tanzir Rahman") and a scoping decision ("no roles or multi-user features"). The NFRs have product-specific bounds: NFR-1 names 1440×1024, NFR-2 gives 1280px, NFR-3 lists concrete behaviours (focus trap, Esc, labels, chart text alternatives) and NFR-4 is an architectural rule you can check. The Vision (§1) is generic as product vision, but its second paragraph ("frontend only… pixel-faithful… swapped for a real API later without rewriting the screens") is the actual thesis, so it earns its place.

### Findings
- **[low]** The §2.1 JTBD overclaims compared with the FRs (§2.1 bullet 4) — "track my progress against them" covers spending targets, which no FR delivers. *Fix:* Reword to match v1 (see the Decision-readiness finding).

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is clear: replicate the designs faithfully on a provider abstraction that can later be replaced. Features follow the screen inventory rather than "easy first", which suits a design-replication release. SM-1 (fidelity) and SM-2 (swappability) map directly onto the two halves of the thesis, and SM-C1 is a real counter-metric against the specific failure mode of hard-coding Figma values for pixel-match. FR-33 and NFR-4 enforce it. This is better than most PRDs at this stakes level manage.

### Findings
- **[medium]** SM-1 leaves out the auth screens (§7 SM-1) — "Validates FR-6 to FR-35 and NFR-1" excludes FR-1 to FR-5, yet screens 101–103 are designed frames that must also match Figma. *Fix:* Change to "FR-1 to FR-35", or say why auth is excluded.
- **[low]** SM-2 cannot be checked as stated (§7 SM-2) — "needs no changes to screen components" can only be shown hypothetically until a real API exists. *Fix:* Add a checkable proxy, for example "a second stub provider (e.g. an empty-dataset provider) can be injected with zero component changes", or a lint/import rule that blocks mock imports from components (which also verifies NFR-4).

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs are concrete and testable: exact column lists (FR-18, FR-19, FR-20), order of nav items and categories (FR-6, FR-22), a gauge formula with a cap (FR-23), validation rules (FR-28, FR-32 "minimum length of 8"), formatting examples (FR-34), and a cross-screen consistency rule (FR-33). Where the PRD falls short, it falls short where downstream stories will feel it most: the core fidelity bar, a chart axis spec that cannot be built, an undefined data relationship, and unbounded list sizes.

### Findings
- **[high]** The fidelity bar has no tolerance (§4.10 NFR-1, §7 SM-1) — "matches its Figma export in layout, spacing, colour and type scale" and "on reviewer sign-off" leave done-ness for the product's main metric to opinion. The font is also uncertain: the addendum says "probably Inter, though the designs don't name it", and §8 does not track this. *Fix:* State a tolerance and a method, for example "overlay against the 2× PNG at 1440×1024, ≤2px deviation on layout and spacing, exact token colours, type scale per addendum". Confirm the font, or add it to §8.2 with an owner.
- **[high]** A logarithmic axis with a "$0" tick cannot be built (FR-21; FR-14 "The same axis is used on Overview") — A log scale has no zero, and the ticks $0/$2k/$10k/$50k/$250k are not evenly spaced in log10 either. Overview weekly values will also be far smaller than the $250k ceiling. Engineers will each pick a different fix. *Fix:* Specify the mapping exactly, for example "piecewise-linear scale with the five design ticks evenly spaced; values interpolate linearly within each band", or "symlog". Say whether the weekly chart uses the same tick values or rescales them.
- **[high]** The Expense vs Transaction relationship is undefined (§3 Glossary; FR-15, FR-22, FR-33; addendum "ExpenseCategory and Expense") — The Glossary says a Transaction "belongs to a category" with kind Revenue/Expense, while FR-22 lists "expense items" and the addendum models Expense as a separate entity. FR-33's rule ("the same record shows the same values on every screen", "each category total is the sum of its expenses") cannot be checked until it is clear whether Expense items *are* Expense-kind Transactions. *Fix:* Add one Glossary line, for example "An Expense item is a Transaction of kind Expense; category totals on FR-15/FR-22 sum Expense-kind Transactions in the month containing Mock today."
- **[medium]** The sign convention for Total Balance is missing (FR-10, FR-33) — "the sum of all account balances" with Account types including Credit Card and Loan, which are liabilities. Whether they add or subtract changes the headline number. *Fix:* State the rule, for example "Credit Card and Loan balances are stored as negative and subtract", or "Total Balance sums asset accounts only".
- **[medium]** List lengths and pagination are unbounded (FR-12 "the next bills", FR-13 "a short list", FR-18/FR-19 "Load More appends the next page") — There are no counts, no page size, no sort order, no "upcoming" window relative to Mock today, and nothing about what happens to Load More when rows run out. *Fix:* Give counts from the designs (e.g. Upcoming Bill shows 2, Recent Transaction shows 5), a page size, sort by date descending (bills ascending from Mock today), and "Load More hides when all rows are shown".
- **[medium]** The Weekly option on Expenses has no definition (FR-21 "The dropdown offers Monthly and Weekly comparison, mirroring FR-14") — FR-14 defines what Monthly shows on Overview, but FR-21 never says what Weekly shows on Expenses (which week, which legend). *Fix:* Add "Weekly renders the FR-14 chart (Sun–Sat of the week containing Mock today, This week / Last week)".
- **[medium]** Revenue and Expense amounts have no display rule (FR-34, FR-13, FR-19) — FR-34 covers decimals and separators but not sign or colour for Revenue vs Expense rows. The phrase "Headline balances … show none" does not say which elements count as headline (Balances card totals? Account Details Balance?). *Fix:* Add sign/colour rules per kind and list the elements that use zero decimals.
- **[low]** The target goal month for the Overview modal is unstated (FR-11, FR-23, FR-25) — The Goals card can select any month, but it is unclear which month's goal the Overview pencil edits. *Fix:* "The Overview pencil edits the goal for the month containing Mock today; Adjust Goal edits the selected month."
- **[low]** Removal side effects are unstated (FR-17) — It does not say what happens to the removed account's transactions, where the user lands after removing from Account Details, or whether Total Balance and the carousel recompute. *Fix:* Add one bullet for each.
- **[low]** Old Password validation is undefined (FR-32) — Under "any well-formed credentials succeed" (§4.1), it is unclear what "Old Password" is checked against. *Fix:* State "non-empty only", or "must match the password used at mock login/signup".

## Scope honesty — strong
This is the PRD's strongest dimension. §5 Non-Goals does real work (for example, "The Welcome Page is not part of the product. It is a Figma cover frame."). Every inert control is named inline with a cross-reference to §8.2 (FR-8 kebab, FR-9 bell and search, FR-16 and FR-18 Add/Edit). FR-26 uses `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` exactly where an over-limit indicator might otherwise be assumed. There are about 25 open items (assumptions plus deferred items), which is fine for a low-stakes replication build because each one has a stated default.

### Findings
- **[high]** Mock-state persistence is left undecided (addendum "in-memory or `localStorage` state"; FR-1, FR-17, FR-25, FR-30, FR-31) — FR-1 requires the session to survive reloads, but the PRD never says whether data edits (removed accounts, goals, profile, avatar "stored only on the client") also survive a reload, or how a reviewer resets the seed. This decides the architecture and the QA approach. *Fix:* Decide in the PRD, for example "All mock mutations persist in localStorage; a reset (e.g. on Logout or a dev control) restores the seed", and tag it `[ASSUMPTION]` if it has not been confirmed.
- **[low]** The appearance of inert controls is unspecified (FR-8, FR-9, FR-16, FR-18) — "Shown but does nothing" could mean an enabled-looking no-op or a disabled style. NFR-3 makes them keyboard-focusable either way. *Fix:* "Inert controls render as designed, stay focusable, and do nothing on activation", or render them with `aria-disabled`.

## Downstream usability — adequate
FR IDs FR-1 to FR-35 are contiguous and unique. §8.2 cross-references (#1–#6) resolve, and the source-screen tags make UX and story extraction easy. The Glossary is present and "Mock today" is a strong shared term. The gaps are a few missing entity definitions and inconsistent screen tagging, both of which story slicing will run into.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Core entities are missing from the Glossary (§3) — Bill, Expense item and User/Profile have no definitions, although the addendum lists them as entities and FR-12, FR-20 and FR-22 depend on them. *Fix:* Add one-line Glossary entries consistent with the addendum entity list.
- **[low]** Screen tags are inconsistent (§4 preamble "Each one references its source screen as `[scr NNN]`") — FR-5 to FR-15, FR-19 and FR-20 have no `[scr]` tag and rely on section headings. *Fix:* Add tags (for example `[scr 104]` on FR-10 to FR-15, `[scr all auth]` on FR-5) so each FR stands alone when extracted.
- **[low]** NFRs sit inside the FR section (§4.10 "Non-functional requirements:") — They are easy to miss when extracting by heading. *Fix:* Promote them to their own section (`## 4.11` or a new top-level section).

## Shape fit — strong
A single-persona, design-replication frontend is correctly shaped as a per-screen capability spec with no user journeys, and the PRD does not add any. The screen-numbered FR grouping fits the Figma source. The cross-cutting FRs (FR-33 to FR-35) catch the rules that a per-screen layout would otherwise scatter. The addendum correctly keeps the stack and tokens out of the requirements ("They are not requirements"). Nothing is over-formalised.

### Findings
- **[low]** Behaviour between widths is unstated (NFR-2) — Fidelity is defined at 1440, the floor is 1280, and nothing covers layout between 1280 and 1440 or above 1440 (fixed-width centred or fluid). *Fix:* One line, for example "content is fluid between 1280 and 1440 and capped at 1440 and centred above that."

## Mechanical notes
- **Screen count is wrong (§6.1):** "All 13 product screens: 101–112, including the 104.1, 110.1 and 110.2 modal states". 101–112 is 12 screens, and the three modal states bring the total to 15 frames (matching `design-reference/screens/` minus the Welcome Page). Correct the count.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip (§9):** This is mostly clean. Gaps: FR-14's inline assumption that "Monthly shows the same chart as Expenses (FR-21)" is only partly indexed (the index covers the dropdown options only). FR-31 "stored only on the client" is indexed but not tagged inline. FR-25 "Esc and overlay click also close" is tagged as an assumption even though NFR-3 makes Esc a requirement, so drop the Esc part from the assumption.
- **Glossary synonyms:** "Category Goal" and "Spending limit" are both defined as the same thing (§3). FR-26 and FR-27 use "spending limit", while the section and modal titles use "Category Goal". Keep one term and alias the other.
- **"Keep me signed in" assumption** is attached to FR-1 in §9, but the "any well-formed credentials" assumption sits in the §4.1 description rather than on FR-1. This is harmless, but the index entry should point to §4.1.
- **FR-9 "Hello {firstName}"**: how firstName comes from "Full name" (FR-30) is not stated (e.g. the first whitespace-delimited token).
- **Required sections:** All sections appropriate to this stakes level and product type are present.
